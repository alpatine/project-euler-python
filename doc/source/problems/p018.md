# Project Euler 18 - Maximum Path Sum I

## Problem Description
The problem reads:

> By starting at the top of the triangle below and moving to adjacent numbers on
> the row below, the maximum total from top to bottom is $23$.
> 
> <p style="text-align: center;">
> <span style="color: darkred; font-weight: bold;">3</span><br/>
> <span style="color: darkred; font-weight: bold;">7</span> 4<br/>
> 2 <span style="color: darkred; font-weight: bold;">4</span> 6<br/>
> 8 5 <span style="color: darkred; font-weight: bold;">9</span> 3</p>
>
> That is, $3+7+4+9=23$.
> Find the maximum total from top to bottom of the triangle below:
> <p style="text-align: center;">
> 75<br/>
> 95 64<br/>
> 17 47 82<br/>
> 18 35 87 10<br/>
> 20 04 82 47 65<br/>
> 19 01 23 75 03 34<br/>
> 88 02 77 73 07 63 67<br/>
> 99 65 04 28 06 16 70 92<br/>
> 41 41 26 56 83 40 80 70 33<br/>
> 41 48 72 33 47 32 37 16 94 29<br/>
> 53 71 44 65 25 43 91 52 97 51 14<br/>
> 70 11 33 28 77 73 17 78 39 68 17 57<br/>
> 91 71 52 38 17 14 91 43 58 50 27 29 48<br/>
> 63 66 04 68 89 53 67 30 73 16 69 87 40 31<br/>
> 04 62 98 27 23 09 70 98 73 93 38 53 60 04 23</p>
> 
> **NOTE**: As there are only $16384$ routes, it is possible to solve this
> problem by trying every route. However, Problem 67, is the same challenge with
> a triangle containing one-hundred rows; it cannot be solved by brute force,
> and requires a clever method! ;o)
> 
> [Project Euler Problem 18](https://projecteuler.net/problem=18)

## Approach
The problem description clearly tells us that we should not find the sum for
every path and select the largest. In fact, the question is only asking for the
largest sum that can be made from a path, it is not asking us for the path
itself. This means we can find a way to generate the largest sum and we don't
necessarily need to remember the path that created that sum.

If we move from top to bottom, we have to make a choice each time to step down a
row: left or right. Which option is better depends on the choices made further
down the triangle. This suggests that if we could make the choices further down
the triangle first, then we would be in a better position to know what choices
to make higher in the triangle. We can do this by working from the bottom to the
top.

## Solution
To solve this, we work from bottom to top. Consider the small example:

<p style="text-align: center;">
3<br/>
7 4<br/>
2 4 6<br/>
8 5 9 3
</p>

Focus on the bottom two rows:

<p style="text-align: center;">
2 4 6<br/>
8 5 9 3
</p>

The following algorithm repeats until we have a single value remaining. For each
number on the second last row of the triangle, replace it with the sum of it and
the largest of the two numbers directly below it. Once all numbers in the second
last row are replaced, discard the last row.

* The 2 could be added to the 8 or the 5. 8 is larger so replace the 2 with
$2+8=10$.
* The 4 could be added to the 5 or the 9. 9 is larger so replace the 4 with
$4+9=13$.
* The 6 could be added to the 9 or the 3. 9 is larger so replace the 6 with
$6+9=15$.

After these replacements have been made and the last row discarded, the triangle
becomes:

<p style="text-align: center;">
3<br/>
7 4<br/>
10 13 15
</p>

Repeating this process, the triangle becomes:

<p style="text-align: center;">
3<br/>
20 19
</p>

One last time produces:

<p style="text-align: center;">
23
</p>

Use the same approach on the larger triangle to arrive at the value of $1074$.

## Python Implementation
``` python
from typing import List

def p18(number_triangle: List[List[int]]) -> int:
    layer_count = len(number_triangle)

    for upper_layer_number in range(layer_count - 2, -1, -1):
        lower_layer_number = upper_layer_number + 1
        for upper_layer_position in range(0, upper_layer_number + 1):
            number_triangle[upper_layer_number][upper_layer_position] += max(
                number_triangle[lower_layer_number][upper_layer_position],
                number_triangle[lower_layer_number][upper_layer_position + 1]
            )
    
    return number_triangle[0][0]
```
